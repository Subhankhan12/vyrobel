## ⚠️ WHAT THIS IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT

⚠️ **THIS IS NOT A PRIVACY POLICY, AND IT MUST NOT BE PUBLISHED AS ONE.** It is the factual record a lawyer
needs *before* drafting one: for every category of personal data the product touches, what is collected, from
whom, why, where it is stored, who else receives it, how long it is kept, how it is deleted, and what legal
basis the code implies. **Every row is cited to `path:line`.**

⚠️ **THE LEGAL BASIS COLUMN IS THE CODE'S IMPLICATION, NOT A LEGAL OPINION.** It says what the processing
looks like from the implementation. Confirming each basis, documenting a balancing test wherever legitimate
interests is claimed, and deciding the retention periods that do not yet exist are the lawyer's work, not
this document's.

⚠️ **THE MOST USEFUL PART IS WHAT IS MISSING.** Where a table holding personal data has **no retention rule
and no deletion path**, that is stated plainly. A policy that promises a retention period the code does not
implement is worse than one that admits the gap — and the gaps are listed in full at the end.

⚠️ **NOTHING HERE IS DEPLOYED.** Vyrobel has never been released: zero git tags across 863 commits, and no
production environment. So every "is collected" below means *"would be collected the moment a real customer
uses this"*, and every third party listed as inert becomes real on the founder's go-live flips.

## THE SHORT VERSION, FOR THE FIRST CONVERSATION WITH COUNSEL

| # | The thing to decide | Why it cannot wait |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ⚠️ **The Article 28 DPA.** | It does not exist. Vyrobel is the **processor** for every guest record its customers put into it, and Article 28 requires the agreement to be in place **before** that processing begins. Already recorded as a go-live blocker. |
| 2 | ⚠️ **The sub-processor list.** | Also does not exist. At least three parties are or will be sub-processors (see Domain 4). A customer's own DPA will ask for this list on day one. |
| 3 | ⚠️ **Retention periods for the tables that have none.** | Several stores of personal data have **no expiry of any kind** — see the gap list. Each needs a period chosen and then implemented, in that order. |
| 4 | ⚠️ **Controller vs processor, per category.** | Vyrobel is the **processor** for guest data and the **controller** for its own customers' account data. The two carry different duties and the policy must not blur them. |
| 5 | ⚠️ **Whether an operator can ever delete their account.** | There is no path — in the app, by command, or documented — that deletes an operator organisation. Cancelling makes the workspace read-only. |
| 6 | ⚠️ **Special-category risk in identity documents.** | A passport image reveals nationality, and may reveal more. The product stores these encrypted and purges them, but the drafting must address Article 9. |

### ⚠️ What this domain cannot answer, and the lawyer will need to

WHAT THE CODE CANNOT TELL A LAWYER (each of these is unanswerable from the repository, not merely undocumented):

1. CONTROLLER vs PROCESSOR. Nothing in the code states who the controller is. Structurally, each tenant organisation controls its guests' data and Vyrobel is a processor; but Vyrobel is a controller in its own right for `login_activity` (explicitly platform-realm-only) and arguably for `webhook_events`. A policy needs this decided. There is no DPA text, no sub-processor list and no processor-instruction mechanism anywhere in app/ or config/.

2. RETENTION PERIODS FOR ALMOST EVERYTHING. There is exactly ONE quantified retention rule in the codebase (LoginActivity::RETENTION_DAYS = 90) plus two hard-coded windows (GuestDeletionService::GRACE_DAYS = 14; the ID purge's --days=30 default). Guests, reservations, folios, payments, messages, reviews, notes, wallet ledger, wishlist/waitlist, audit_events, domain_events, webhook_events and booking_quotes are ALL retained indefinitely with no code-level rule. A policy cannot state a retention period for any of them from this code; the founder must set them.

3. THE SCHEDULER IS A SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE FOR TWO GDPR PROMISES. The 14-day guest erasure (`guests:process-deletions`) and the 30-day ID-document purge (`guest-documents:purge-expired`) are ordinary daily scheduled commands. Both depend on one crontab line (`* * * * * php artisan schedule:run`) that the project's own docs flag as not yet installed (deployment pending). If that line is missing, both promises silently never run and nothing in the app reports it. Do not let a policy promise a deletion timescale until the cron is verified in production.

4. JURISDICTION AND THE ID-DOCUMENT OBLIGATION. The code calls guest ID capture 'the legal guest register' but names no statute, no country and no obligation. It is also OPTIONAL in the wizard (`id_photo` is nullable), which is inconsistent with a legal-obligation basis. Which jurisdiction, which law, and is it mandatory?

5. SPECIAL-CATEGORY DATA IS UNMODELLED. Free-text fields routinely carry health data — the codebase's own test fixture for notes_internal is 'Nut allergy', and the check-in prompt invites allergy notes. There is no Art.9 flag, no separate access control, no explicit-consent capture and no distinct retention for it.

6. WHETHER THE ERASURE GAPS ARE ACCEPTED OR OVERSIGHTS. Nine stores about a guest are NOT cleared by Guest::anonymize(): reservations.cancellation_reason + reservation_status_history.reason + the matching domain_events payload; tags/taggables and the tag string inside audit_events/domain_events; abandoned booking_quotes (with a code comment pointing at a purge that does not exist); guest_credit_ledger.description; wishlist/waitlist/email-preference rows; an operator's notifications.data.context carrying the guest NAME (ThreadAssignedNotification); webhook_events.payload (OTA customer name/email/phone in plaintext, and whatever Stripe sends); audit_events.ip/user_agent captured from the GUEST when the guest is the actor; and maintenance-ticket note bodies. Only ONE of these — reviews.body — is a documented, deliberate decision. The rest read as oversights against the project's own stated rule.

7. THE STRIPE PAYLOAD CONTENTS. I verified the mechanism (the entire event body is persisted verbatim via $event->toArray()) but I did NOT observe a live Stripe event, so I cannot enumerate which guest fields Stripe actually includes (receipt_email, billing_details.name/email/address are the likely candidates). Someone must inspect a real `webhook_events` row from the live account before the policy describes what is stored.

8. PROVIDER-SIDE RETENTION AND TRANSFERS. Hostinger mail — since 2026-09-25, not Postmark (email content + recipient addresses), Stripe (cardholder data, guest email collected on their hosted page), and — when it goes live — Channex (OTA guest identity) all hold guest data outside this system. The code contains no retention terms, no transfer mechanism (SCCs/adequacy) and no processor agreement reference for any of them. Whether US transfers occur is not determinable from the code.

9. WHO CAN SEE WHAT, AS A ROSTER. I verified the perimeter rule (contact PII renders on the Guests CRM only; owners and the OAuth API get name-only) and the route gates, but I did NOT audit every operator role's effective permission set, and I did NOT verify the perimeter by rendering pages in a browser. A policy claiming 'only staff with role X can see your phone number' needs that role-by-role check.

10. NO GUEST-FACING DATA EXPORT (Art.15/20). Owners have a 'Request my data' export; guests have none. Guests can download per-booking PDFs only. A subject access request for a guest would have to be serviced manually, and no code path assembles one.

11. THE UNCLAIMED-GUEST PROBLEM. Most guests will never claim a portal account, and every self-service control except the marketing unsubscribe sits behind `auth:guest`. In practice their erasure, access and rectification rights depend entirely on the operator acting. The policy must say how such a request is made.

12. WHAT I DID NOT CHECK. I did not run the test suite; I did not open any page in a browser; I did not inspect live table CONTENTS (only schema); I read vendor/ only for the session handler; I did not audit Domain 2+ subjects (operator/owner/staff/applicant data, owner banking, platform-admin data) beyond where they touch guest records; and I did not review docs/ for existing privacy statements — every finding above is from code, migrations, config and routes."

### ⚠️ What this domain cannot answer, and the lawyer will need to

WHAT A LAWYER NEEDS THAT THE CODE DOES NOT ANSWER.

1. ⚠️⚠️ THERE IS NO PRIVACY NOTICE, TERMS, COOKIE POLICY OR DPA — ANYWHERE. Verified by grepping the MECHANISM, not the words: no route, no controller method, no view. `grep -rn "Privacy Policy|privacy-policy|Data Processing|DPA" resources routes app` returns ZERO hits, and the marketing route group (routes/web.php:552-598) registers product/features/modules/how-it-works/pricing/try-booking/switching/for-hotels/for-apartments/for-villas/example-scenarios and nothing legal. So there is no document to which ANY of the legal bases above could be disclosed, and no Art.13/14 information is given to staff, owners, invitees or the operators themselves. Every "legal_basis" I give is what the code IMPLIES; none is stated anywhere in the product.

2. ⚠️⚠️ THE CONTROLLER/PROCESSOR SPLIT IS NOT UNIFORM, AND THE CODE DOES NOT MODEL IT AT ALL. The brief's framing ("Vyrobel is the CONTROLLER here") holds for the operator ORGANISATION as customer, for the owner as counterparty, and for Vyrobel's own platform admins. It does NOT cleanly hold for operator STAFF: the roster, shifts, leave requests, leave reasons, role assignments and property assignments are HR data the OPERATOR determines the purpose of — Vyrobel is arguably the processor. Yet the SAME natural person's login account, sessions, IP history, 2FA secret and passkeys are data Vyrobel controls. One person, two roles, one `users` row, no distinction in code or docs. This determines who owes the Art.13 notice, who answers an Art.15 request, and who is liable — and nothing in the repo decides it.

3. ⚠️ NO RETENTION POLICY EXISTS FOR ANY CATEGORY EXCEPT ONE. Exactly one enforced retention rule exists in this entire domain: `login_activity` at 90 days (`LoginActivity::RETENTION_DAYS`, `platform:prune-activity`, scheduled daily). Everything else has NONE — verified structurally, not by absence of grep hits: there is no `Prunable`/`MassPrunable` trait anywhere in app/, and routes/console.php contains ten scheduled entries of which only that one is a privacy prune. Silent categories, ranked by exposure: `audit_events` (permanent per-action IP + user-agent history of every named person) · `webhook_events.payload` (raw provider bodies) · `trusted_devices` + `login_approvals` (IP + UA, revoked/consumed but never deleted) · `export_downloads` · `shifts` and `leave_requests` (working-time + possible health data) · `owner_invites` / `owner_team_invites` (emails of people who never accepted) · `password_reset_tokens` (no `auth:clear-resets`) · `failed_jobs` (no `queue:prune-failed`) · Passport token tables (no `passport:purge`) · `notifications` · `operator_task_states` · `messages`. And note the operational fragility: even the one working prune depends entirely on the single crontab line `* * * * * php artisan schedule:run` that docs/DEPLOYMENT.md flags as load-bearing.

4. ⚠️⚠️ THERE IS NO ACCOUNT-DELETION OR STAFF-OFFBOARDING PATH AT ALL — this is the answer to the brief's direct question, and it is a hard no. No code anywhere deletes a `User` (verified: the only `forceDelete()` calls in app/ are guest ID documents). `anonymize()` exists solely on `Guest`. There is no "remove staff member" route: routes/web.php offers only role assign/revoke. There is no organisation-deletion path either. What EXISTS is three status flips — owner self-deactivate → `status='disabled'`; operator owner-removal → linked user `status='inactive'` + owner soft-delete; owner-team removal → `status='disabled'` — every one of which explicitly retains all data. A departed staff member therefore keeps, indefinitely: name, email, phone, password hash, encrypted 2FA secret and recovery codes, passkeys, trusted-device rows with IPs, membership, roles, property assignments, every shift, every leave request including its free-text reason, and every audit row they generated with IP and user-agent. THE TENSION THE BRIEF ANTICIPATES IS REAL AND UNRESOLVED: the audit trail's immutability is a genuine Art.32/accounting justification for the ACTION record — but it is not a justification for the `ip` + `user_agent` columns on every row, nor for the plaintext `users.email`, nor for `leave_requests.reason`, nor for `export_downloads.downloader_name`. No one has drawn that line, and drawing it is a product decision, not a refactor.

5. ⚠️ `leave_requests.reason` IS THE SINGLE MOST LEGALLY EXPOSED FIELD IN THE DOMAIN. A 160-char plain varchar, unvalidated as to content, that a staff member fills in to explain why they need time off. The placeholder says "Annual leave"; the field accepts "hospital appointment". That is Art.9 health data with no Art.9(2) condition, no encryption, no minimisation, no retention limit and no deletion path. The containment that DOES exist (visible only to `schedule.manage`; excluded from the roster grid and the schedule PDF via RosterWeek.php:133) is genuine and well-built — but it bounds who reads it, not that it is collected and kept forever. 6. ⚠️ THE ENCRYPTION POSTURE IS INTERNALLY INCONSISTENT FOR THE SAME PERSON. `owners.email/phone/tax_id/vat_number` are encrypted casts; `users.email/phone` are plaintext varchar. ## 🔴 The five that are GDPR-material today

### 1. ⚠️ A CODE COMMENT DEFERS A GDPR CASE TO A MECHANISM THAT DOES NOT EXIST

`Guest::anonymize()` — the certified erasure path — states at `app/Models/Guest.php:349-351`:

> *"an abandoned quote has no guest link at all (audit F-2b-07), so those are cleared by **the scheduled

⚠️ **There is no scheduled retention purge.** The eight scheduled commands are enumerated in
`routes/console.php:15-72` and not one touches `BookingQuote`; no console command references the model at
all. So an abandoned booking quote — carrying a prospective guest's **encrypted email, phone and a
`guest_snapshot`** — is retained **indefinitely** and is **unreachable by erasure**, while the code reads as
though the case were handled.

⚠️ This is the most dangerous shape a gap can take: not an absence, but an absence with a comment saying it
is covered. Anyone auditing `anonymize()` would tick this box.

### 2. 🔴 NO ACCOUNT DELETION EXISTS FOR ANY HUMAN ACCOUNT

There is no path — in the app, by console command, or documented — that deletes an operator, an owner, a
staff member or an organisation. `User` declares `SoftDeletes` (`app/Models/User.php:45`) but **nothing calls
it**. Cancelling a subscription sets `readonly_from` and deletes nothing
(`app/Services/SubscriptionLifecycleService.php:44-53`). Owner "removal" is an archive that keeps the
financials, and the code says so plainly. **A customer who asks to be deleted cannot be, today, except by
hand on the database.**

### 3. ⚠️ THE PERMANENT STORES HOLD IP ADDRESSES AND THIRD-PARTY PAYLOADS, AND ERASURE DOES NOT REACH THEM

- **`audit_events`** — every row carries `ip` and `user_agent`. **No prune, no TTL, permanent by design.**
  Erasure appends a row here; it removes nothing.
- **`webhook_events.payload`** — verbatim provider bodies, which for an OTA booking contain the guest's
  **name, email and phone**. No prune. Not touched by `anonymize()`.
- **`domain_events`** — no prune; consumed rows are never removed.

⚠️ The project's rule that values must never be written into `audit_events.before/after` **does hold** — that
was checked and confirmed. The exposure is the request metadata and the third-party payloads, not the rule.

### 4. ⚠️ BACKUPS UNDO DELETION

`ops/backup.sh` takes an **unfiltered `mysqldump`** retained for 14 days (`BACKUP_RETAIN_DAYS`). A restore
reinstates data that erasure removed. This is normal and defensible — but it must be **disclosed**, and the
14-day window is the real outer bound on any erasure promise.

### 5. ⚠️ POSSIBLE ARTICLE 9 DATA WITH NO RETENTION AND NO DELETION PATH

Staff **leave requests carry a free-text `reason`**. A reason for time off is frequently health information,
which is a special category under Article 9. There is **no prune and no delete route** — approve and deny are
status updates. An approved 2026 leave request and its reason persist forever.
